## End of Tenancy Cleaning Checklist: Room-by-Room Guide

Hopelessly scrubbing an oven the night before handing the keys back is a memorable way to lose sleep, and maybe your deposit. A focused, room-by-room approach removes guesswork, prevents last-minute panic, and gives you the best chance of passing the final inspection without disputes.

This end of tenancy cleaning checklist lays out exactly what to clean, how to prioritize tasks, and which problems warrant professional help. Read it as a sequence you can follow over a few days, or use it as a final pre-handover run-through. It covers kitchens, bathrooms, living areas, bedrooms, windows, floors, and the small details that tenancy managers notice most.

## How to use this end of tenancy cleaning checklist

Begin early, and stage your work. Do deep cleaning tasks like oven degreasing and carpet spot treatment two to three days before move-out so surfaces have time to dry and odors can dissipate. Save quick touch-ups and the final inspection walkthrough for the day you hand over the keys. Keep before-and-after photos and receipts for any professional services you hire.

Before you start, gather supplies. Having everything at hand speeds the process and prevents back-and-forth trips.

Essential cleaning supplies (short list)
- All-purpose cleaner, degreaser, and bathroom cleaner
- Microfiber cloths, scrub brushes, sponge, and scouring pad
- Mop, bucket, vacuum with attachments, and carpet stain remover
- Rubber gloves, garbage bags, and a step ladder
- Oven cleaner and limescale remover for bathrooms and taps

With supplies ready, follow the room-by-room sections below. Each section highlights common problem areas and practical techniques to get them landlord-ready.

## Kitchen: the make-or-break room

Landlords and inventory clerks scrutinize kitchens. Grease, burnt-on food, and neglected appliances trigger the most deductions.

Start with appliances. Remove racks and trays from the oven and soak them in hot, soapy water. Apply an oven cleaner according to the product instructions, leave it to work, then scrub and rinse thoroughly. Wipe the interior with a damp microfiber cloth to remove residue. Clean the hob, paying attention to burner surrounds and knobs. If you have a gas hob, remove and soak metal trivets; for electric hobs, use a non-abrasive cleaner to avoid scratches.

The fridge and freezer require emptying and defrosting if needed. Remove all shelves and drawers and wash them in warm, soapy water. Wipe the interior with a solution of warm water and a small amount of bicarbonate of soda to neutralize odors. Leave the fridge doors open for a while to dry and air out.

Cabinet surfaces, both inside and out, collect grease. Empty cupboards and wipe interiors, starting at the top and working down. Pay attention to hinges and handles where grime accumulates. If the tenancy inventory listed any fittings, return them to their original position.

Sinks and taps often show soap scum and limescale. Use a limescale remover or a paste made from baking soda and water for stubborn deposits. Rinse and shine taps with a microfiber cloth. Check waste disposal units and traps for odors; clean or replace filters as necessary.

Worktops, splashbacks, and tile grout should be cleaned and rinsed. For grout that has darkened with age, use a grout brush and a cleaning solution designed for tiles. If stains persist, note them and photograph them, as older grout discoloration is sometimes considered wear and tear.

Finish with floors. Sweep to remove debris, then mop using an appropriate floor cleaner. If the kitchen floor shows sticky residues or scuff marks, a second pass may be necessary.

## Bathroom: targets for inspection

Bathrooms are judged harshly because mold and limescale can indicate neglect. Clean systematically from top to bottom.

Begin with ceilings and light fittings. Remove any cobwebs and dust before proceeding to tiles and grout. For mildew around the shower and bath, apply a mold remover and leave it to act for the recommended time. Use a grout brush to agitate stained areas.

Showerheads and taps require descaling if the water supply is hard. Soak small removable parts in a vinegar solution, or use a commercial descaler for fixed fittings. Replace shower curtains or liners if they are heavily stained or mildewed; if the curtain is renter-supplied, washing may suffice.

The toilet needs thorough attention. Use a toilet brush and a toilet cleaner to scrub the bowl, rim, and under the seat. Wipe the outside surfaces and base, then check behind the toilet, where dust and hair collect. Clean the cistern, if accessible, and tighten any loose toilet seats.

Bathroom mirrors and glass show water spots. Use a glass cleaner or a vinegar solution and a microfiber cloth to avoid streaking. Clear soap scum from sinks and baths with a non-abrasive cleaner. Rinse and dry all surfaces to leave a polished finish.

## Living room and communal areas: fresh and neutral

Your living areas should look cared for and neutral. Dusting, vacuuming, and removing marks will go a long way.

Dust ceiling corners, light fittings, and picture rails. Clean radiators by vacuuming between fins and wiping external surfaces. If there are built-in units, empty and clean shelves, and wipe any scuff marks off skirting boards and doors.

Soft furnishings like sofas and curtains retain odors and stains. If the tenancy agreement requires professional upholstery or curtain cleaning, arrange this a few days ahead so items can dry. For small sofa stains, use a fabric cleaner according to the furniture label, and always test in an inconspicuous spot.

Floors make a strong impression. Vacuum carpets thoroughly and treat visible stains with a carpet cleaner. For heavily soiled carpets, hire a professional carpet cleaner. Hard floors should be swept and mopped. Buffing wooden floors after cleaning restores a good shine.

## Bedrooms: simple but thorough

Bedrooms must feel move-in ready. Clean wardrobes, drawers, and dust under beds.

Remove and wipe shelving, paying attention to any adhesive residue from stickers or tape. If landlords expect holes to be filled, gently fill small holes and sand smooth before painting the repaired patch to match the surrounding surface, if required by tenancy terms.

Wash window sills, clean inside and outside of windows if safe to do so, and check curtains or blinds for dust and stains. Vacuum or mop floors last, and remember to clean the inside of light fittings and switch plates.

## Windows, blinds, and door frames

Windows receive scrutiny because they affect perceived overall cleanliness. Clean both sides of windows, frames, and tracks. Window tracks trap grime and require a small brush or an old toothbrush plus soapy water to clean effectively. Remove dust from blinds with a damp cloth and deep clean fabric blinds according to manufacturer instructions.

Doors and frames often have fingerprints and scuff marks. Wipe down doors, clean around door handles, and pay attention to the bottom of doors where shoes can leave marks.

## Floors and carpets: stains, odours, and wear

Carpets are expensive to replace, so treat them carefully. Start with a thorough vacuum to remove loose dirt. For isolated stains, blot rather than rub and use a suitable stain remover. For deep or set-in stains, professional cleaning is usually the safer option.

If inventory requires a professional clean, book a trusted cleaner with a certificate. Keep receipts and a copy of the report to show the landlord.

Hard floors vary by material. Wooden floors need a cleaner designed for wood, and excessive water can damage boards. For tile and vinyl floors, a stronger cleaner and a gentler mop technique will restore shine.

## Walls, skirting boards, and small repairs

Large holes or structural damage can be more than normal wear and tear, but small nail holes and scuff marks are often expected to be repaired. Fill small holes with lightweight filler, sand smooth once dry, and paint if necessary and permitted by the tenancy terms. If you repaint, use a neutral colour that matches the original.

Wash walls where grease, grime, or handprints appear, starting with a diluted all-purpose cleaner. Skirting boards collect dust and paint splatters. Clean them with a damp cloth and attend to chipped paint where appropriate.

## Outdoor spaces, balconies, and shared areas

If your tenancy includes a balcony, garden, or shared hallway, treat these spaces with the same attention as interior rooms. Remove debris, sweep surfaces, and clear gutters or drains if accessible and required. Store and remove any rubbish or leftover items before the final inspection.

For communal areas that you frequently used and were responsible for, make sure to leave them as you found them. Sweep porches, wash down outdoor furniture, and remove cigarette butts or pet waste.

## Final walkthrough and documentation

The last hour should be dedicated to a systematic walkthrough. Start at the front door and move through the property, testing lights, taps, and appliances. Take photographs of each room in good daylight, showing cleaned surfaces and any features that might otherwise be questioned.

Record meter readings and keep a copy of the inventory check-out sheet. If possible, arrange the final inspection with the landlord or inventory clerk present, and be prepared to point out recent professional cleaning receipts. A calm, organized presentation often resolves small disputes.

Common final checks include ensuring all keys are returned, appliances are empty and defrosted, bins are clean and removed, and curtains or blinds are hanging properly.

## When to hire professionals

Professional cleaning becomes worth the cost when tasks require specialized equipment or carry high financial risk if done poorly. Examples include heavily soiled carpets, an oven with baked-in grease that will not respond to consumer cleaners, and deep mold remediation in a bathroom.

Compare quotes and check reviews. Ask for a written checklist from the company that shows what they will clean and whether they provide a satisfaction guarantee. Keep invoices for deposit disputes.

## Practical tips that save time and disputes

Start the day with the kitchen and bathrooms, then move to living areas and private rooms. Work from top to bottom so dust and debris fall onto uncleaned floors. Use hot water where safe, and let cleaning solutions work for the recommended time before scrubbing.

If you discover damage or stains beyond cleaning, document them immediately and notify your landlord. Open communication reduces the chance of surprise deductions. When in doubt about the tenancy agreement terms, check your contract or ask the property manager before making irreversible repairs.

## Final thoughts

A clean, well-documented handover reflects responsibility and makes reclaiming your deposit far more likely. This end of tenancy cleaning checklist breaks the job into manageable parts and highlights the spots landlords check first. Tackle the toughest areas early, keep receipts for any professional work, and leave the property orderly and neutral. That calm, prepared approach will save time, money, and stress on moving day.
