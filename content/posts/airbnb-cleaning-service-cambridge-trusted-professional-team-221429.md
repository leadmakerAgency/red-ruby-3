What a trusted professional team delivers
A trustworthy cleaning service does more than tidy up. They operate with clear procedures, trained staff, and quality control that you can audit. For short-term lets, a professional team typically offers:

- Consistent turnover cleans that follow a checklist so no detail is missed.
- Linen and laundry management, including stain treatment, timely repossessions, and coordinated pickups if off-site laundry is used.
- Deep cleans on a scheduled basis, covering oven, behind appliances, upholstery and curtains, and descaling where required.
- Restocking of consumables such as toiletries, coffee, and basic household supplies, either from your stock or theirs on a replenishment agreement.
- Rapid response for last-minute turnovers and emergency cleans when a guest departs late or leaves a mess.
- Inspections with photographic evidence, report notes, and communication about maintenance issues they discover.
- Inventory control and simple maintenance reporting, so lightbulbs, heaters, and plumbing are flagged before guests notice.

How to choose the right cleaning team
Choosing the right provider comes down to five practical dimensions: reliability, quality assurance, insurance and vetting, communication, and fit with your operational model.

Start with references and reviews, but go deeper. Ask for a client list and request to speak with at least two current hosts whose properties match your size and style. Request proof of public liability insurance and written confirmation that staff have undergone background checks where relevant. Ask about training: how are cleaners taught to treat Airbnb properties differently from standard domestic cleans? Reliable teams will describe procedures for turnovers, handling lost items, and using a checklist backed by photographic checks.

Make sure the team’s capacity matches your calendar. A single cleaner might be adequate for a one-bedroom flat with 24 hours between guests. If you manage multiple properties or frequently face same-day turnovers, choose a team that offers rotation of staff, spare capacity, and coverage for sickness or holidays. Confirm whether the company supplies cleaning materials and linens, or whether you must keep a stock. Both arrangements are common; each has trade-offs for cost, control, and storage.

What a typical turnover clean includes
A useful way to evaluate services is to know what tasks belong in a standard turnover versus a deep clean. A standard turnover clean prepared for guest arrival usually covers:

- Strip and remake beds with fresh linen, check mattresses and pillows for stains, and rotate cushions and protectors as needed.
- Clean bathrooms thoroughly, descaling taps, mirrors, shower screens, and clearing hair from drains, followed by sanitising high-touch surfaces.
- Vacuum and mop floors, paying attention to edges and under furniture, and remove visible allergens and debris.
- Wipe down kitchen surfaces, clean appliances used by guests, empty and sanitise the refrigerator, and ensure dishware and cutlery are spotless.
- Remove trash and recycling, replace liners, and check exterior bins where applicable.
- Dust surfaces, picture frames, light switches, remote controls, and entry areas, and ensure curtains and blinds are tidy.
- Restock essentials such as hand soap, toilet paper, tea, coffee, and basic cleaning sprays according to your agreed allowance.
- Perform a final inspection, photograph rooms, and report any damage or missing items.

Deep cleans, scheduled monthly or seasonally, expand on this list. They include oven and extractor fan cleaning, descaling kettles and shower heads, laundering curtains and mattress protectors, polishing furniture, and treating grout and stains. For fabrics and antiques, the team should recommend specialist treatment rather than attempting aggressive cleaning.

Pricing and scheduling: what to expect
Pricing for cleaning services varies depending on property size, condition, and required turnaround time. Simple one-bedroom turnovers in Cambridge often fall at the lower end of the scale, while larger houses or properties that need laundry handling will be costlier. Same-day turnovers and short notice cleans attract premium rates. Deep cleans are typically quoted as separate jobs.

Be clear about what is included. Some companies quote a base rate with optional add-ons for linen service, consumables, or heavy duty stain removal. Others offer all-inclusive packages that roll everything into one monthly invoice. Choose what matches your priorities: a la carte pricing gives more control, while bundled services remove small management tasks from your plate.

When scheduling, build realistic buffers between bookings. Even with an efficient team, allow time for unexpected items such as maintenance fixes, lost keys, or guests who delay checkout. For high turnover properties consider midday checkout and late afternoon check-in windows for the next guest to give cleaners adequate time. If your calendar shows back-to-back bookings frequently, discuss standby or priority slots with your cleaning provider at a negotiated rate.

Practical steps to improve outcomes and reduce costs
A cleaning team’s effectiveness improves when hosts prepare properties for turnover. Simple host-side actions reduce time cleaners spend on avoidable tasks and lower your per-turnover costs. For example, supply a clear checklist on the property for guests to follow at checkout. Encourage them to dispose of major food waste, place dirty dishes in the dishwasher, and pile used towels in a designated basket. Clear guest instructions cut down on mid-clean surprises.

Standardise supplies and storage. If you expect cleaners to launder and restock linens, provide a dedicated storage closet with labelled items and a simple inventory sheet. That reduces time spent hunting for replacements and ensures consistent presentation. Keep a small maintenance fund for fast small repairs such as replacing burnt-out bulbs or tightening loose fittings, so these do not delay turnover acceptance.

Use photographic proof and short reports. A brief set of standard photos taken by cleaners after each turnover becomes a record for guest disputes, damage claims, and quality control. Require a short form of report that notes any issues needing attention or replacement.

Handling guest damage, late checkouts and disputes
Even the best systems encounter occasional damage or late departures. A professional cleaning service will report damage immediately, providing photos and a timestamp. Ensure your provider understands your policy on guest damage and reporting thresholds. For late checkouts, agree on a standard escalation process, such as a 30-minute grace window followed by an extra fee request to the guest. If a late checkout prevents a same-day incoming guest, document the cause and keep all communication with the guest for dispute resolution.

The team begins with linen removal and starts a load in the on-site washer while one cleaner focuses on bathrooms and another vacuums and dusts. With a 4:00 PM check-in time, the team leaves a comfortable buffer for the host or property manager to do a final spot-check or to resolve the few lingering issues.

Questions to ask before you sign a contract
Before you commit, ask these practical questions: What does a standard turnover include, and what counts as an extra? How are cleaners vetted and trained? Are cleaners employed directly or contracted? Do you provide insurance certificates and DBS checks? How do you handle linen, and are they picked up and dropped off? What is your response time for emergency cleans? Can I require photographic proof after each job? How are prices adjusted for peak times or same-day turnovers? A professional provider will answer these plainly and provide written terms.

A long-term relationship reduces mistakes, ensures staff familiarity with your property, and often delivers better pricing and priority during busy periods.
