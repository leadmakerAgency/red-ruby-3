## What an Airbnb turnover clean should include

Short-term rental cleaning is not a standard weekly tidy. It blends hotel-style presentation with the practicalities of a home. A solid turnover covers four layers.

First, a fast, thorough clean of kitchens and bathrooms. That means degreasing hobs, sanitising high-touch points, descaling taps if needed, scrubbing the loo, and leaving glass and chrome streak-free. In the kitchen, bins are emptied and lined, the fridge checked for leftovers, and the dishwasher loaded or emptied.

Second, bedroom resets. Beds are fully stripped and remade with the correct linen set per bed size. Pillows are fluffed, throws straightened, and under-bed areas checked for lost chargers or socks. If you stock spares, the cleaner should rotate them to balance wear.

Third, common spaces and detail work. Floors are vacuumed and mopped, mirrors and windows spot-cleaned, rugs shaken out, and crumbs lifted from sofas. Remote controls, light switches, handles, and keys get a quick disinfectant wipe. Toiletries are replenished, coffee and tea restocked, and welcome cards refreshed.

Finally, verification. Good Airbnb cleaners work from a checklist that matches your listing and they send time-stamped photos. Pictures of each room, the linen closet, and any reported damage create a record and save you from guesswork when a guest mentions a missing hairdryer or a scuffed wall.

Rates vary by property size, linen setup, and how often you book. Two markets use two currencies and slightly different patterns.

In Cambridge, UK, a one-bedroom flat usually falls in the £55 to £85 range for a standard turnover that includes a clean and a bed change. Add £10 to £25 for each extra bedroom or bathroom. If your cleaner handles laundry on-site, factor in time for washing, drying, and ironing, since this can add £20 to £45 depending on loads and machine speed. In Cambridge, Massachusetts, the same one-bedroom turnover often ranges from 110 to 160 dollars. Larger homes run higher, with frequent surcharges during peak move-in weekends, leaf-peeping season, and graduation weeks when schedules tighten and parking is scarce. Linen handling and supply restocking can add 25 to 60 dollars per visit, depending on the number of beds and your par level.

Urgent or same-day calls tend to carry a premium in both cities. So do properties with heavy turnover or those that require additional services like patios swept after a storm, grill cleaning, or de-icing steps in winter.

## Scheduling without stress

The calendar is where great cleaners earn their keep. Hosts in both Cambridges face bursty demand, driven by university dates, conferences, and tourism. Build in three practices that keep turnovers on time.

Share your booking calendar. Give your cleaner read-only access through your platform or export an iCal feed. Mark checkouts and check-ins clearly and keep notes about early arrivals.

Lock in time windows. Most teams work in fixed routes. If your checkouts are 11 a.m. and arrivals are at 3 p.m., agree on a buffer for late checkouts and a protocol for priority tasks if a guest lingers. Some hosts allow cleaners to start bedrooms first while the last guest packs in the lounge.

Plan for access and parking. Key safes near the entrance are worth their weight in reviews. In Cambridge, UK, several streets require permits, and college-area restrictions can be strict. In Cambridge, MA, resident-permit streets and snow emergencies can upend plans. Tell your cleaner where to park, how to access permits if available, and what to do if a car blocks the drive. Small details prevent big delays.

## Linen, laundry, and restocking

Turnovers fail more often on linen than on bathrooms. Decide early how you will handle sheets, towels, and supplies.

On-site laundry works if your machine is fast and you have at least two full sets per bed. Three sets are better during high season. Keep a small iron or a garment steamer for pillowcases and set aside a folding table for speed. Expect to top up energy usage and plan the schedule to avoid bottlenecks in the dryer.

Off-site laundry is faster for back-to-backs. Some cleaning companies include pickup and drop-off with next-day turnaround. Others partner with local laundries. Linen rental shifts the entire task to a provider and can be cost-effective if you prefer hotel-standard sheets without purchasing them.

For restocking, standardise your supplies. Keep refills in a labeled cupboard or a lockable owner’s closet. Many cleaners will maintain a short inventory list after every visit. That makes it easy to budget for toiletries, dish tabs, bin liners, tea, coffee, and welcome chocolates. Agree on an approval threshold, such as notifying you when stock falls below a one-week par.

## Onboarding your cleaner for smooth turnovers

Even the best cleaner cannot guess your priorities. A short, specific onboarding makes everything easier and pays for itself in calmer Saturdays.

Start with a one-page playbook. Map the rooms, bed sizes, and any odd quirks, like the tricky latch to the garden door or the thermostat that resets. Add arrival and departure windows, Wi-Fi details, and a contact number for emergencies. Label the linen closet and show where to find backup toiletries, spare bulbs, batteries, and a plunger.

Create a photo checklist. For each room, include two or three reference photos of how it should look when ready. Ask your cleaner to upload matching photos after each turnover. That gives you a quick glance review and a dated record you can reference if a guest mentions an issue.

Agree on damage protocol. Cleaners often spot problems first. Set rules for what they should do if they find a broken lamp, missing remote, or stained rug. Many hosts provide a petty cash float or Revolut or Venmo for small purchases such as a replacement corkscrew. Bigger items should trigger a message with photos so you can decide on claims.

Run a trial turnover. Before your calendar gets hectic, schedule a full clean and reset with your cleaner. Walk through the checklist, note timing, and refine the order of operations. If you plan to leave reviews that mention cleanliness, make sure the presentation meets your standard.

## Quality control without hovering

Once your system is live, you can maintain quality from a distance.

Ask for timestamped photos. A quick gallery of the kitchen, bath, beds, and supplies verifies the essentials. If your listing has a balcony, hot tub, or barbecue, include photos of those too.

Audit in person once a quarter. Walk the space between guests every few months, then schedule a deep clean. Rotate mattresses, run a limescale treatment, clean inside the oven, and touch up paint on skirting boards and door frames.

Bundle maintenance. Pair cleaning visits with simple checks, such as battery tests for smoke and CO alarms, GFCI test button checks in the bathroom, and a faucet run in little-used sinks. The small routines prevent bigger problems.

Provide feedback while it is fresh. Praise specifics, like perfect grout lines or a pullout sofa that looked brand new. Be just as clear about misses. The more concrete your notes, the faster a team adapts.

## A sample turnover timeline

To visualise a back-to-back day, imagine a two-bedroom flat near Parker’s Piece or a condo off Massachusetts Avenue with 11 a.m. checkout and 3 p.m. arrival.

At 11:05 a.m., the cleaner enters via lockbox. They strip both beds and start the first laundry load if on-site. Bins are emptied, and the dishwasher is run. Bathrooms are sanitised top to bottom. While floors dry, they stage bedrooms with fresh linen, then return to the kitchen to wipe cabinets and the fridge. Last, they vacuum and mop the hallway and lounge, set out welcome items, and capture photos. If laundry is off-site, they swap used linen into a labeled bag and pull a fresh set from the closet. By 1:30 p.m., everything is staged and they send a completion message with photos. You still have a buffer if a previous guest left a stubborn stain that needs spot treatment.
