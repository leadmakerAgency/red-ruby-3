Move Out Cleaning Service: Checklist, Costs & Top Tips

June 7, 2026

Most tenants lose part of their security deposit over something as small as a greasy oven or scuffed baseboards. A reliable move out cleaning service can turn a stressful, last-minute sprint into a smooth handover that protects your deposit and frees your time for the next chapter.

Introduction

Moving is messy and expensive. Packing, logistics, and emotional farewells compete for your attention while the landlord expects a spotless unit at the final inspection. Hiring a professional move out cleaning service removes a major burden and increases the odds of a full deposit return. This article explains exactly what these services do, how much they cost, how to choose the right provider, and concrete tips you can use right away to avoid common mistakes.

Why a move out cleaning matters

Landlords and property managers use move-out inspections to assess repairs and cleaning needs before the next tenant arrives. If the property looks unkept, you may receive deductions for professional cleaning or repairs. A proper cleaning addresses the things landlords notice first: counters and sinks, oven and refrigerator interiors, bathrooms, floors, and visible dust or cobwebs. Beyond deposit recovery, a professional cleaning shortens the turnover time for the property, which benefits both you and the next occupant. For renters who are short on time or moving long distances, using a move out cleaning service converts a logistical headache into a predictable service with clear deliverables.

What a move out cleaning service typically includes

Providers vary, but most reputable move out cleaning services include a comprehensive room-by-room process. In kitchens cleaners will wipe down all surfaces, degrease cabinets and range hoods, clean the oven interior and stovetop, and defrost and sanitize the refrigerator. Bathrooms receive a deep scrub of showers, tubs, toilets, sinks, tile grout, and mirrors. Living areas and bedrooms get dusting from baseboards to light fixtures, window and sill cleaning, vacuuming and mopping, and spot cleaning of walls where appropriate. Additional attention goes to high-touch areas such as doorknobs, switches, and vents. Many companies also remove cobwebs, clean blinds, and leave windows streak-free.

Some firms advertise a standard clean that covers visible surfaces and a deep clean that includes oven racks, inside cabinets, inside the fridge, and hard-to-reach grime. Carpet shampooing, upholstery cleaning, and exterior window washing are often treated as add-ons because they require specialized equipment. Confirm which items are included before booking, because misunderstandings about scope cause most end-of-lease surprises.

Room-by-room checklist you can expect from professionals

Kitchens: Clean and degrease countertops and backsplashes, clean sink and faucet, wipe cabinet exteriors and clean visible interiors, scrub oven interior and stovetop, empty and defrost refrigerator and wipe interior, clean microwave inside and out, mop and sanitize floors.

Bathrooms: Scrub tub, shower and grout, descale and clean showerhead and fixtures, sanitize toilet inside and out, clean sink and vanity, polish mirrors, mop floors and wipe baseboards.

Living rooms and bedrooms: Dust all surfaces and fixtures, clean window sills and tracks, vacuum carpets and rugs, mop hard floors, wipe baseboards and doors, spot-clean walls where allowed.

Miscellaneous: Remove cobwebs, clean light fixtures and ceiling fans, clean vents and radiators, wipe closet interiors, sweep or clean balconies and patios if included in your agreement.

Costs and what affects price

Move out cleaning service pricing depends on property size, condition, geographic location, and selected add-ons. National averages range widely. For a standard one-bedroom apartment, expect a flat fee between $100 and $200 in many markets. Two-bedroom units typically run $150 to $300. Large homes, heavy soiling, or rush bookings push prices higher. Hourly rates apply for some teams, usually between $25 and $50 per cleaner per hour. Hourly models can be cost-effective for smaller, tidy properties. Flat-rate packages remove uncertainty and work well when you want a predictable cost.

Other factors that increase price include carpet deep-cleaning, interior window washing above ground level, oven and refrigerator deep cleans, hoarder-level debris removal, and excessive pet hair. Travel distance for the crew and same-day bookings also raise rates. Always clarify whether materials like cleaning supplies and equipment are included. A reputable move out cleaning service will provide a written estimate that lists included tasks and potential extras.

Saving money without sacrificing results

You can reduce the final bill by handling a few simple tasks before the cleaners arrive. Remove and dispose of unwanted items, empty trash, and collect loose personal belongings. Sweep patios and clear out bulky debris, and if you can, defrost the freezer to speed up refrigerator cleaning. These small efforts let the crew focus on deep cleaning, rather than hauling and sorting, which can keep the cost lower.

How to choose the right move out cleaning service

Start by checking company reviews and asking for recent references. Look for firms that provide written, itemized quotes and that guarantee their work for a short period after cleaning. Confirm whether the company carries liability insurance and workers compensation, so you are not liable if a cleaner is injured while on your property. Ask if the team performs background checks for employees if that matters to you.

Request clarity on supplies and equipment. Some companies bring their own tools and eco-friendly cleaners, while others expect you to provide basic items. If you have allergies or prefer green products, be explicit about that requirement. Also ask about their satisfaction policy: will the company return to address any missed spots, and is there a time window for such callbacks? A reliable move out cleaning service will answer these questions without hesitation and will include clear terms in a contract.

Timing and scheduling best practices

Book the cleaning at least one to two weeks in advance for peak moving months such as late spring and summer. Schedule the cleaning after most of your boxes are out but before the final walk-through. If possible, arrange for completion a day before the landlord’s inspection so you can handle last-minute touch-ups. For long-distance moves, provide a reliable contact and consider leaving keys with the property manager or the company, following local legalities and building policies.

If you need cleaning on the same day as move-out, expect to pay a premium. For multi-stage moves where you will tile or paint after the cleaners, coordinate with contractors so cleaning follows final repair work, not precedes it.

DIY versus hiring professionals

For a small, tidy studio you might save money by doing the cleaning yourself, provided you have the time and stamina. However, cleaning an entire house while packing for a move is a strain and often results in missed details that landlords note. Professionals bring experience and equipment that achieve a consistent result quickly. For renters who need a spotless finish to secure a deposit, or for sellers preparing a home for market, hiring a move out cleaning service is generally the better investment.

Consider a hybrid approach if budget is a concern. Do basic decluttering and trash removal yourself and hire the professionals for deep cleaning tasks such as carpets, ovens, and bathrooms. That combination often yields the best value.

Common add-ons and hidden fees to watch for

Carpet steam cleaning, interior window washing, oven and refrigerator deep-clean services, and trash removal often cost extra. Some companies charge for excessive pet hair removal or for properties they classify as hoarded. Travel fees apply if the job is outside a company’s service area. Demand a written estimate that calls out extras and ask how they classify unusually dirty properties. A clear scope prevents surprises.

Preparing the property so cleaners can be efficient

Before the crew arrives, remove all valuable and personal items, empty trash bins, and unplug and empty appliances if possible. If you have left-over paint, patch small nail holes and wipe down smudges so cleaners can focus on a thorough job rather than repairs. Move furniture that you want cleaned under or around, or tell the company whether lifting heavy furniture is included. Provide access instructions and parking details. Clear communication and a short pre-clean tour or photograph set will help the team prepare and deliver the expected service.

Documenting the condition for your own protection

Take dated photos of each room before and after cleaning, focusing on trouble spots such as the oven, refrigerator, floors, and walls. Keep copies of the move out cleaning service contract and receipts. If the landlord deducts cleaning fees, present your documentation. Many disputes resolve quickly when you can show before-and-after images taken on move-out day.

Handling the final inspection and disagreements

If the landlord identifies issues after cleaning, ask for a written list or photographs that indicate what remains unsatisfactory. If you used a professional move out cleaning service, contact them immediately and request a callback service if their policy covers re-cleaning. If the landlord claims damage rather than soiling, request an itemized repair estimate. Maintain calm and professionalism during disputes. Often, a walk-through with the cleaning company present clears up misunderstandings and leads to a prompt resolution.

Real-world examples

A couple leaving a two-bedroom apartment in a major city booked cleaning three days before their landlord inspection. They removed personal items and scheduled the cleaners for the day after they emptied the unit. The crew spent four hours deep-cleaning the kitchen and bathrooms and charged a flat fee plus a small travel premium. Photos taken before and after the service satisfied the landlord, and the couple received their full deposit within two weeks.

In another case a renter attempted to save by cleaning herself the day before a long-distance move. Exhausted and pressed for time, she missed grime in the oven and a stained area under the refrigerator. The landlord deducted a significant amount for a professional oven and floor cleaning. If she had hired a move out cleaning service, those details would likely have been addressed, and the deposit would have been preserved.

Safety, insurance, and green cleaning options

Confirm that the company uses safe chemicals and methods, especially if pets or children will be present when you hand over keys. If you prefer low-VOC or fragrance-free products, request them in writing. Insurance matters because accidents happen; a worker may slip on a wet surface or damage a fixture. Hiring a company with liability and worker protection relieves you of potential legal exposure.

Final checklist template you can use the night before move-out

Walk through the unit and ensure all personal items and trash are gone, appliances are emptied and unplugged when possible, minor repairs such as loose screws or small holes are fixed, and keys and access information are organized. Confirm the cleaner’s arrival time and contact details. Take final photos. These steps streamline the cleaning process and help avoid last-minute charges.

Conclusion

A well-chosen move out cleaning service takes a major burden off your plate and increases the chances of recovering your full deposit. Understand what the service includes, how pricing works, and which extras might apply. Prepare the property before the crew arrives, document the condition with photos, and choose a company with clear policies and insurance. With a little planning, you can turn one of the most stressful parts of moving into a managed, predictable step that lets you focus on the next move.
